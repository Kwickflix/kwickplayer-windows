## What it is

Kwick Player is a player for **your own** TV service. It is not a subscription
and it comes with nothing to watch — you bring what you already pay for:

- an **Xtream Codes** login from your provider,
- an **M3U playlist** (a web address or a file on your PC),
- a **media server** on your own network — Jellyfin, Emby or Plex,
- or a **KwickTV** sign-in, if you're a KwickTV member.

Any of those work, and you can have several at once. No source is treated as
more important than another.

This is the **same app as the Android version**, running on Windows — same
screens, same settings, same everything, laid out for a keyboard and a mouse
instead of a remote.

## What you get

- **📺 Live TV & a full TV Guide** — channels by group with logos, search and
  favourites, and a guide built from your provider's listings or from an XMLTV
  guide of your own.
- **🎬 Movies & series** — poster grids by category, detail pages with artwork,
  cast and plot, seasons and episodes, with a tick and a progress bar on
  everything you've watched.
- **▶️ Continue Watching** — picks up where you left off, and offers the next
  episode of a series rather than the one you just finished.
- **🖥️ Media servers** — a Jellyfin, Emby or Plex library sits in the same grids
  as everything else and plays straight off your own machine.
- **👤 Profiles** — separate watch state, favourites and settings per person,
  with an optional PIN.
- **🔄 KwickSync** — KwickTV members: favourites, lists, watch state and settings
  follow you between this PC, your phone and your Roku.
- **⭐ Favourites & lists** — star anything, and group things into named lists of
  your own.
- **⏺️ Recordings** — record a live channel to a file, now or on a schedule.
- **🔲 Multi-View** — up to four things playing at once in a grid, with the sound
  on whichever one you click, within whatever your provider's line allows.
- **💬 Subtitles & audio** — subtitles and closed captions styled how you like
  them, plus audio-track picking and a preferred language.
- **🔒 Parental controls** — hide categories outright, or lock them behind a PIN.
- **🎨 Themes** — a wardrobe of complete looks, including a terminal-style one.
- **🏟️ Sports** — a fixtures view that finds the game first, then finds the
  channel in your own playlist showing it.

## How to get it

There is **nothing to install**. You download one zip, and the folder inside it
*is* the app.

1. Download the zip and **unzip it anywhere** — your Desktop, a folder of your
   own, a USB stick. (Right-click the file → **Extract All**.) Keep all the
   files together in the one folder.
2. Open the **Kwick Player** folder and run **`KwickPlayer.exe`**.

No setup wizard, no administrator rights, and nothing is copied anywhere else on
your PC. To keep it handy, right-click `KwickPlayer.exe` and pin it to Start or
to your taskbar.

Because nothing is installed, Kwick Player is **not** in the Start menu and
**not** in **Settings › Apps** — there's nothing there to find and nothing to
uninstall. To remove it, delete the folder.

**New versions.** The app tells you when there's one and opens your browser so
you can fetch it; it never downloads or installs anything by itself. Unzip the
new one and **use the new folder** — don't unzip it on top of the old one, which
is how you end up with a half-and-half copy. Your settings, sign-in, watch
history and recordings are kept outside the folder (see below), so the new copy
picks them all up. Delete the old folder once you're happy with the new one.

> **Windows will warn you the first time.** It will most likely show a blue box
> headed **"Windows protected your PC"**. Click **More info**, then **Run
> anyway**. It only asks once for this copy.
>
> **Why it asks:** Windows recognises programs that have been stamped with a paid
> certificate, bought from one of the companies that sell them. Kwick Player is
> not stamped, so Windows says it doesn't recognise it and asks you to confirm.
> It is not telling you something is wrong with the app, and nothing about the
> app changes when you click **Run anyway** — it's the same Kwick Player before
> and after. You'll see the same box the first time you run a new version, for
> the same reason.

## Keyboard & mouse

Everything works with the mouse, and the keyboard gets you around faster:

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **Escape** | Go back. In a video: leaves full screen first, then leaves the player. It never closes the app by accident. |
| **/** | Jump straight into Search with the cursor ready — Search is in the left menu as well, just under Home |
| **Space** | Pause / play |
| **← / →** | Jump ten seconds back or forward through a film, episode or recording |
| **↑ / ↓** or the **mouse wheel** | Volume |
| **M** | Mute |
| **F** or **double-click the picture** | Full screen — and again to come back out, to the same window you had |
| **Tab** | Move through the screen; reaches the left menu and comes back out again |

**Getting out of a video with the mouse:** a **back arrow** sits in the top-left
corner of the picture, and the mouse's **back button** (the thumb button, if
yours has one) does the same thing.

The window remembers its size and position for next time.

The player's button bar is laid out for a PC, so two buttons you may know from
the phone aren't on it: there's no full-screen button (double-click, or **F**),
and the picture shape lives in **Settings › Playback › Picture shape** instead —
it's a settle-once choice on a window you can resize.

## Where your stuff lives

- **Settings, sources, profiles, favourites and watch history:**
  `%APPDATA%\Kwick LLC\Kwick Player`
- **Recordings:** `Videos\Kwick Player`, unless you point
  **Settings › Library › Recordings folder** somewhere else.

None of that is inside the unzipped folder, which is why you can throw the folder
away, or replace it with a newer one, without losing anything.

### Removing everything

Deleting the folder leaves your settings and your recordings alone on purpose —
unzip a copy again and you pick up exactly where you were. If you want it all
gone:

1. Delete the unzipped **Kwick Player** folder. That is the whole app gone.
2. Paste `%APPDATA%\Kwick LLC\Kwick Player` into a File Explorer address bar and
   delete that folder.
3. Delete anything you don't want from `Videos\Kwick Player`.

> Step 2 wipes your saved sign-in and your watch history as well, and it cannot
> be undone.

**One extra, only if you were trying Kwick Player early on.** Very early builds
wrote their diagnostic log into your own Documents folder by mistake. Look for
`Documents\KwickPlayer` — if it is there, it holds nothing but that old log file
and you can delete the whole folder. Nothing writes to it any more, so if you
don't have one, you never will.

## Not in this first release

- **It is not on the Microsoft Store.** Download it from here (or from
  [kwickplayer.tv](https://kwickplayer.tv/)) — that's the only place it comes
  from.
- **Double-click a playlist file.** Kwick Player opens `.m3u` and `.m3u8`
  files — double-click one and it's added to your sources and shown in Live
  TV. Windows won't hand over a file type another app already owns, so if
  double-clicking still opens VLC or Media Player, choose Kwick Player once
  under **Settings › Apps › Default apps** (there's a button for it in
  **Settings › Sources › IPTV Sources**).
- **No sleep timer in the player.** It's there so a phone stops playing after
  you've fallen asleep; a PC already sleeps on its own schedule.
- **Recordings need the app left open.** On a phone the recording keeps going in
  the background; on a PC, closing the window ends it. Close the window while
  one is running and the app asks first whether to keep recording or stop.
- **One video engine.** The phone app can switch between two players; only one of
  them exists on Windows, so the "Player engine" setting doesn't offer a choice
  here. Nothing else changes.
- **No brightness control in the player.** That's a phone screen thing — use your
  monitor.

## Something not working?

1. In the app, open **Settings › About › Export Logs**. That sends a diagnostic
   log straight to us — no files to find, no attachments.
2. It then shows you a short **Log Key** (six characters, with a Copy button).
   Copy it down.
3. Tell us what happened in **Discord** or **Telegram** (below) and give us
   that Log Key — it takes us straight to your log.

Please say which version of Kwick Player you're on (**Settings › About**) and
what your source is (Xtream, M3U, media server, or a KwickTV sign-in).
